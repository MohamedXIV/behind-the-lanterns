# GDD 01 — Game Vision and Player Experience
**Status:** initial design · **Updated:** 2026-10-29

## Player fantasy
You are a free young stable worker: not a criminal, chosen hero, monarch, or professional fighter. You can clean, carry parcels, tend animals, earn small sums, take paid errands and journeys, become a courier, make friends, rent a room, acquire a home and land, trade or grow produce, and eventually own a traveling boat-home. The intriguing possibility is that a routine errand leads behind the familiar lights into a wholly unexpected story.

The stable is a **starting point**, never a permanent role lock. The protagonist's appearance is fixed for the early releases.

## Two mutually reinforcing loops
**Everyday loop:** wake/rest → choose work, social, livelihood, preparation, or exploration → earn money/relationships/knowledge → maintain or improve living conditions → continue at your own pace.

**Adventure loop:** notice a hint **or** find something without a hint → prepare and travel → explore, bargain, evade, or face dangers → bring back stories, discoveries, materials, recipes, seeds, and opportunities → expand everyday life.

**Design shorthand:** *Adventure unlocks livelihood; livelihood funds adventure.* It is a design direction, not a requirement to grind one loop to enter the other.

## Agency
- No mandatory main quest, forced career, or final boss.
- Optional extended storylines may end while the wider sandbox remains.
- The player can be adventurous, cautious, charitable, mercantile, or domestic, within believable consequences.
- Not everything extraordinary is supernatural or hostile, and not every discovery requires combat.
- No place secretly needs a quest flag simply to exist.

## Game feel
Warm city streets, stalls, lanterns, animals, roofs, inns, harbors, and households contrasted with distant voyages, unsafe shores, forbidden buildings, strange creatures, and unsettling legends. Humor, tenderness, and mysterious unease coexist without turning into a horror or combat-first game.

### Core verbs
Move, observe, talk, ask, remember, hide, flee, negotiate, carry, trade, tend, craft, prepare, rest, travel, explore, dodge, block, roll, and occasionally fight.

## Player growth
Start with little property and modest means. Progression comes from:
- Better housing (stable room → rented inn room → owned house/land).
- Learned practical skills and small skill branches.
- Knowledge and trust earned in the world.
- Access to transport and, eventually, a personalized boat that can serve as a home.
- New resources, livelihoods, and authored opportunities encountered through travel.

No permanent magic-power escalation and no character appearance editor in early builds.

## Success criteria
Players should be able to ignore quest prompts, make their own goals, genuinely discover an unadvertised secret, choose not to fight, and feel that returning home with a new story matters.

## Anti-goals
Farming-first clone; MMO-scale continent; endless randomized busywork; aggressive live-service retention; crime-simulation combat against authorities; rigid quest-chain lockstep; mandatory heroic ending.
